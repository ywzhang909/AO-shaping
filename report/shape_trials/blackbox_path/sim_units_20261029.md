The fitted gain itself is a property of the bench, so only the ratio is comparable.
- **Reproduced -- the anchor level.** On Mono12 frames the model's absolute scale sits at **1.000x** the anchor with `count_rate` and at **16.00x** with the old normalisation -- the ~16x that makes the model difference, not the measurement, drive `anchored_intensity`.
- **Reproduced -- the implied loss change.** The anchored prediction says -10.32% with the old normalisation and -2.73% with the fix, for a measured change of -3.26%. The fix lands within 0.53 percentage points of the measurement; the old one is off by 7.07.
- **Not reproduced -- the hardware outcome (negative result, left as found).** `paired_decision` accepted **3/3** blocks with the fix and **3/3** with the old normalisation on the matched checkpoint, and **3/3** vs **2/3** with the +10 % defocus error. The simulated loop does not reproduce the bench's 0/N rejection, and no parameter was tuned to make it do so.
- **What the mismatch actually corrupts** is the *reading*, not always the verdict. In the mismatched scene the old normalisation predicts +4.93% (worse) where the frames measured -3.26% (better) -- a sign flip -- and one of three blocks is then rejected. A block that still agrees by luck is not evidence that the anchor was right; on this bench the 16x error moved the predicted change by 6.05 percentage points under a checkpoint defect the fit would consider acceptable.
